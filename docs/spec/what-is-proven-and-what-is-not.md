# What is proven and what is not

*Which claims have evidence at their actual size?*

The product at a glance · Status: built and tested locally

A working screen is only the start of evidence. The team reports a deployed reader and five passages generated by the event-account pipeline with Ruth. The browser checks ran with sound muted, so they show layout and scripted behavior, not what a listener hears. A separate local timing exercise on ReadWorks audio turned up difficult words and suspect times. It is useful because it did not pass cleanly.

The research claim is narrow too. The supplied research report supports helping students access grade-level text. It found no ReadWorks study testing this read-along audio against no audio on a learning measure, and none testing lit words. The large descriptive study it cites looked at grade-level assignments and answers, not reading growth or this product. A prototype cannot turn that access rationale into improved reading scores.

**The event-account pipeline has published passages; listening, measured sync, integration, and learning outcomes remain open.**

## Where it stands

ReadWorks reviewed an earlier themed demo and praised Ruth from an earlier pipeline in a separate account. It has not seen focused mode. The team reports the current stack deployed in the event AWS account in `us-west-2`, with five passages generated, audio served through CloudFront, and a repeat passage avoiding regeneration. Direct storage access returned 403 in an earlier check. The live library lists 14 passages, including a 1,435-word and a 2,046-word passage with Ruth as their only voice; one passage lists 46 words to know. No real device, child, teacher, or production ReadWorks trial is recorded. The teacher demo uses made-up students. The evidence table is as of November 4, 2026.

## Technical details

| Major part or claim | Evidence now | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Focused reader and long-passage layout | 41 of 41 Edge checks, muted, on The Big Hike and both long passages at six sizes (`frontend-src/tests/focused_browser.mjs`). The same test, run against the live site on Nov. 4, passed 42 of 42 guard rules, muted. | Audible sync, real device accessibility, child comprehension, or ReadWorks acceptance. |
| Classic reader | 162 of 162 muted browser checks (`frontend-src/tests/reader_layout_browser.mjs`). | Listening quality or student preference. |
| Word replay and controls | Reader engine and local browser harnesses exercise seeking, play controls, and display states. The team reports live audio loading and the clock running in a headless browser. | Live tap-a-word is not verified. Highlight tolerance was not measured. |
| Long-passage timing | Local WhisperX timed 1,423 of 1,435 words in one long passage and 2,019 of 2,046 in another; remaining tokens are punctuation such as `***`, `[...]`, quotes, and dashes. | A clean publish: “Brrrrr-boppppp!” is estimated at about two seconds after recognition heard other words; “8” has a suspect 20 ms. The local hold check would stop both partly because of recognition errors. Audio is outside the repo. |
| Narration synthesis and AWS stack | The team reports the CDK stack deployed with `generativeVoice=true` and five Ruth passages generated by the pipeline. The default deployment remains Joanna. | Audible quality or consistent pronunciation. |
| Machine validation and optional heard-word gate | `tests/test_audio_check.py`, `tests/test_quality_gate.py`, and `tests/test_reader_bridge.py` cover local rules with fixtures or fake AWS. | A human listening approval or a false-hold rate on the catalog. The gate is off by default. |
| Publication contract | Reader bridge writes audio, document, and index in that order; local fake-service tests cover failures. The team reports live audio and guide clips loading through CloudFront and direct storage access returning 403 earlier. | Cache behavior after regeneration in another voice; the new guide paths omit voice and are cached for a year. |
| New word-help audio | `lambdas/syllable_audio/handler.py` builds whole-word, per-syllable, and slow clips, marked `needs human check`; the team reports playback in a muted headless browser on the Ruth deployment. | Quality by ear, correct syllable splits, and behavior when one word fails and its guide entry has no audio. |
| Teacher demo | 173 of 173 browser checks on one machine; 172 of 173 on another, where a standard-reader word-card check missed. Cause unknown; older local media is possible. | Real classroom roles, privacy fit, or reliable behavior on all setups. |
| Cost | The pipeline's cost metric (`lambdas/worker/audio_check.py`) estimates Polly at $16 per million characters for neural, counting the audio and speech marks requests, and $30 for generative. It is an estimate, not a bill. | A measured AWS bill. Transcribe rate in `us-west-2` is unconfirmed; the design doc assumes $0.006/minute and a higher rate is possible. |
| Learning benefit | the team's research report supports grade-level access as a rationale and describes connected-topic routines separately. | Reading growth, comprehension gain from Sound Along, or a benefit from lit words. |

The live setup reportedly has the quality gate and reader bridge on and passage API off. The legacy student API remains; keyboard operation and comparing two voices were not verified. The site is public to anyone who has its address, and legacy `/generate` can start paid runs without a credential. There is no human approval before generated audio could reach students. Useful local commands: `npm run build`, `npm run test:frontend`, `python tests/test_audio_check.py`, `python tests/test_quality_gate.py`, and `python tests/test_reader_bridge.py`. Browser scripts require a served site and local licensed media; do not count muted runs as audible tests.
